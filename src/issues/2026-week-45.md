    title: "Treasury shelved a plan that would have cut the crypto travel-rule threshold by more than 90%."
    summary: >-
      FinCEN and the Federal Reserve Board jointly withdrew the 2020 proposal on
      November 6, filing the notice with the Federal Register. The rule would have
      lowered the point at which institutions must collect and pass along customer
      data on cross-border transfers from $3,000 to $250, and would have written
      convertible virtual currencies explicitly into the Recordkeeping and Travel
      Rule. FinCEN cited the five-plus years since publication and changes in the
      crypto industry as reasons to seek fresh public input. When it proposed the
      rule, the agency argued that illicit actors were splitting funds into smaller
      transfers; Coin Center countered that a $250 line would expose ordinary
      payments to warrantless surveillance. The move follows Treasury's October
      withdrawal of separate proposals covering unhosted wallets and crypto mixing.
    consequence: >-
      The $3,000 threshold remains in force, and any future change for digital
      assets would have to begin again as a new proposal.

  - category: Regulation
    title: "Rules covering crypto at broker-dealers and in 401(k) plans landed at the White House two days apart."
    summary: >-
      The SEC's proposal, which would amend broker-dealer net capital, customer
      protection and recordkeeping rules for crypto assets, reached the Office of
      Information and Regulatory Affairs on November 3. The Labor Department's final
      rule on fiduciary duties in choosing 401(k) investment options arrived
      November 5. Both are designated economically significant. The Labor rule
      carries out President Trump's August 2025 executive order on alternative
      assets in retirement plans; its March proposal offered fiduciaries a legal
      safe harbor for following a documented process that weighs fees, liquidity,
      valuation and three other factors, without requiring any plan to offer crypto.
      Neither text is public, no completion date is set, and Senators Bernie Sanders
      and Elizabeth Warren have pressed the department against opening 401(k)s to
      crypto.
    consequence: >-
      White House clearance is the last interagency step before the Labor rule can
      be published as final and the SEC proposal can be released for comment.

  - category: Banking
    title: "Bhutan's King gave a new central bank the power to hold Bitcoin alongside conventional reserves."
    summary: >-
      A Royal Decree announced November 2 converts the Gelephu Financial Services
      Office into the Gelephu Central Bank, an autonomous authority over monetary
      policy, reserves and financial regulation in the Gelephu Mindfulness City
      special administrative region. The decree permits digital-asset investment but
      does not require it, and no allocation has been announced. Bhutan pledged up
      to 10,000 BTC, mined with surplus hydropower, to the city in December 2025;
      asset manager 3iQ has run a mandate on part of that treasury since July, with
      its size undisclosed. How much remains is disputed. Arkham-tracked wallets tied
      to state investor Druk Holding & Investments fell from about 13,000 BTC in
      October 2024 to roughly 1,750 BTC by June 2026, while DHI says it still holds
      well above 10,000 BTC across custody and trading accounts.
    consequence: >-
      Bhutan now has a central bank whose mandate explicitly covers digital assets,
      operating alongside the Royal Monetary Authority, which remains the national
      currency issuer.

  - category: Research
    title: "A Bitcoin Core developer sketched a way to make quantum thieves forfeit their own coins."
    summary: >-
      Cory Fields of MIT's Digital Currency Initiative posted the idea to the
      bitcoin-dev mailing list on November 4. Anyone spending coins with exposed
      public keys would first lock matching collateral from a quantum-safe wallet
      for a waiting period, such as 30 days. Owners would prepare in advance by
      creating a quantum-safe "landmine" holding hidden proof of ownership;
      revealing it during the lock returns their coins and awards them the
      attacker's collateral. Lost coins, including those attributed to Satoshi
      Nakamoto, get no protection, and honest holders moving vulnerable coins would
      face the same collateral requirement. Fields called it a thought experiment —
      there is no BIP or code.
    consequence: >-
      The design keeps vulnerable coins spendable and relies on an economic penalty
      rather than a protocol-level ban, but it depends on quantum-safe wallets that
      Bitcoin does not yet support.
