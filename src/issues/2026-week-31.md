  - category: Research
    title: "A Firmware Build Error in Coldcard Wallets Let Someone Drain $38 Million From 474 Bitcoin Wallets in 25 Minutes"
    summary: >-
      Introduced in firmware version 4.0.0 in March 2021, the error caused
      affected Coldcard devices to fall back to MicroPython's software random
      number generator during seed generation instead of using the dedicated
      hardware entropy source — producing seeds with as little as 40 bits of
      entropy rather than the expected 128. Seeds derived from predictable
      parameters — device serial numbers and internal clock values — gave an
      attacker a keyspace narrow enough to precompute private keys and scan the
      blockchain for matching outputs at scale; the July 31 sweep targeted 474
      single-signature wallets that had been inactive for extended periods. The
      affected models include Mk3 devices running firmware 4.0.0 through
      5.0.3, Mk4 and Mk5 devices before version 5.6.0, and Q devices before
      version 1.5.0Q. Coinkite urged all potentially affected users to generate
      new seeds with substantial external entropy — a minimum of 50 dice rolls,
      it said, to push keys outside the attackable range.
    consequence: >-
      The attack establishes that a hardware wallet's security guarantee ends
      at the quality of randomness used during seed generation — a property
      invisible during normal operation and unauditable without on-chain
      forensics after funds move.

  - category: Research
    title: "Foundry Put Bitcoin's Most Contested Soft Fork to a Hashrate-Weighted Miner Vote With the August 9 Deadline Approaching"
    summary: >-
      The vote — weighted by each miner's average hashrate from July 6 to
      July 15, with non-responses counted as "No" — would commit Foundry to
      switching its signaling only if supporters cleared 51% of participating
      hashrate; Foundry controls approximately one-third of Bitcoin's global
      hashrate. BIP-110, titled the Reduced Data Temporary Softfork and
      authored by developer Dathon Ohm, reached Complete status in the BIPs
      repository on June 25; its seven rules would restrict most transaction
      outputs to 34 bytes, OP_RETURN to 83 bytes, and data pushes to 256 bytes
      for one year, targeting Ordinal inscriptions, BRC-20 payloads, and other
      non-payment data. Signaling support stood near 2.64% of global hashrate
      as of July 27, with OCEAN Pool signaling by default; Antpool and ViaBTC
      have not moved. Michael Saylor published a 110-point public case against
      the proposal on July 18, and leading Bitcoin Core developers have not
      endorsed the change — leaving the activation path advancing almost
      entirely against developer consensus.
    consequence: >-
      If Foundry's customers vote against signaling and major pools hold out,
      total support will remain well below the lock-in threshold, and BIP-110's
      August 9 window will resolve to a failed activation — delivering the
      clearest live data point yet on how Bitcoin's governance handles a
      contested soft fork under widespread opposition.
