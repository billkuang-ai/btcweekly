    title: "The Supreme Court left the Federal Reserve free to keep a crypto bank off its payment rails."
    what: >-
      The justices denied certiorari on November 16 in Custodia Bank v. Federal
      Reserve Board of Governors, No. 26-62, without comment. That leaves standing
      the Tenth Circuit's 2–1 ruling from October 2025 that Reserve Banks have
      discretion over master accounts even for legally eligible institutions; the
      full court refused rehearing in March. Custodia, a Wyoming special purpose
      depository institution, applied to the Kansas City Fed in October 2020 and
      was turned down in January 2023 over a business model the Fed called
      narrowly focused on crypto. The six-year fight is over. Other routes remain:
      Kraken's banking arm became the first crypto company to win a master account
      in March 2026, and the Fed has proposed a slimmer payment account that would
      pay no interest and offer no discount-window access.
    why: >-
      Fed payment access for crypto-focused banks now depends on Reserve Bank
      discretion and Fed rulemaking, with little judicial recourse left in the
      Tenth Circuit.

    title: "BlackRock's Bitcoin fund had its worst day on record in the same week filings showed institutions owning more Bitcoin ETFs than ever."
    what: >-
      Investors pulled $541 million from the iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) on
      November 19, topping the $528.3 million record set on January 30; all U.S.
      spot Bitcoin ETFs lost a combined $582 million that day. November
      withdrawals reached $3.47 billion, leaving the funds about $3.11 billion net
      negative for 2026, per SoSoValue data cited by Cointelegraph. The other side
      sits a quarter back. Galaxy Research's review of third-quarter 13F filings
      found institutions held a record 356,491 BTC through U.S. spot ETPs on
      September 30, after adding 49,229 BTC — more than they sold in the previous
      three quarters combined — with Wells Fargo now the largest filer at 30,553
      BTC. Those filings are a quarter-end snapshot: by November 16, total fund
      holdings had already fallen 38,531 BTC to 1,252,139 BTC.
    why: >-
      Bitcoin ETF ownership is moving toward wealth platforms and advisors, which
      held 40.5% of 13F-reported holdings, their highest share since launch, while
      hedge funds' share fell to 19% from 50.6% at the end of 2024.

  - category: Research
    title: "A proposed post-quantum signature for Bitcoin got its first full security write-up as securities regulators warned firms against waiting."
    what: >-
      Blockstream researcher Mikhail Kudinov released a 131-page analysis of
      SHRINCS, dated November 18, that ties the scheme's security to the
      properties of its underlying hash function. The trade-off is size: its
      stateful signatures run 548 to 4,619 bytes and its stateless fallback 5,777
      bytes, against 64 bytes for today's Schnorr signatures. The estimated
      121.4–122.7 bits of classical security rest on three unproven conjectures,
      the authors say the work has not been sufficiently peer reviewed, and the
      SHRINCS BIP remains a draft open for comment. Reusing a one-time key on the
      stateful path could let an attacker forge a signature. A day later, a
      63-page IOSCO report said machines able to break current financial
      cryptography could arrive within five years and found few firms testing
      quantum-resistant protections.
    why: >-
      Bitcoin developers now have a hash-based signature candidate with published
      security bounds and stated open problems to review, as the global body of
      securities regulators presses the financial industry to plan its own
      migration.
