    title: "Block filed for an OCC national trust bank charter — putting Cash App's Bitcoin custody infrastructure in line for a single federal supervisor to replace its patchwork of state licenses."
    summary: >-
      Builders Bank & Trust, N.A., disclosed September 8, would operate as an
      uninsured national trust bank: no deposits, no loans, custody and fiduciary
      services only for Bitcoin, stablecoins, and related digital assets. Cash App
      processed $10.7 billion in Bitcoin volume in 2025 and served two million
      active crypto users per month through the second quarter of 2026; the OCC
      application would fold more than 50 state custody licenses into one federal
      charter. The OCC received 40 new-bank applications in the 18 months
      preceding August 2026, of which 23 involved digital-asset activities —
      parallel applications from Ripple, Circle, and BitGo are already in queue.
      A former Northern Trust and BNY Mellon executive was named Builders Bank's
      first president and CEO.
    consequence: >-
      One federal charter eliminates per-state compliance overhead and positions
      Block to compete for institutional custody mandates that currently require
      the legal certainty of federally supervised infrastructure.

  - category: Regulation
    title: "Senate Republicans released a revised CLARITY Act five days before a cloture vote that could advance Bitcoin's permanent commodity classification to a full Senate floor debate."
    summary: >-
      The revised 630-page bill, posted September 10, added more than 114
      provisions requested by Senate Democrats while narrowing DeFi provisions to
      cover only spot and cash digital-commodity transactions. Under the bill,
      Bitcoin is classified as a digital commodity under CFTC jurisdiction; the
      revision adds a requirement that "non-decentralized" trading protocols —
      platforms where individuals or groups can materially alter system
      operations — register with the CFTC. The September 15 cloture vote requires
      60 senators to proceed; Democrats have not collectively endorsed the bill
      despite the added provisions, and an ethics section barring federal officials
      and their spouses from issuing digital assets remains a contested flashpoint.
      No floor vote is scheduled should cloture succeed.
    consequence: >-
      Should the September 15 cloture vote clear 60 senators, the bill would
      advance to the first full Senate floor debate on comprehensive crypto market
      structure legislation — permanently assigning Bitcoin to CFTC jurisdiction
      rather than leaving its classification to agency-by-agency interpretation.
