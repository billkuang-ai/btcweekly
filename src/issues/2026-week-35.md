    title: "Banking Regulators Formally Defined 'Unsafe or Unsound Practice' — Ending the Examiner Latitude That Enabled Crypto Debanking"
    summary: >-
      On August 27, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency and the Federal
      Deposit Insurance Corporation jointly published a final rule giving "unsafe or
      unsound practice" its first statutory definition in U.S. banking law: conduct
      that deviates from generally accepted prudent operational standards and has
      either already caused, or could reasonably cause, material financial harm to
      an institution or significant risk to the Deposit Insurance Fund. Before this
      rule, the phrase carried no legal definition; examiners used that ambiguity
      from 2022 onward to classify bank relationships with cryptocurrency firms as
      categorically problematic — the mechanism the industry called "Operation
      Chokepoint 2.0." Under the new framework, examiners may still issue
      supervisory observations about non-financial weaknesses, but corrective action
      authority is limited to findings tied to material financial risk or regulatory
      compliance. The rule takes effect 60 days after Federal Register publication.
    consequence: >-
      Requiring examiners to anchor corrective action to documented material
      financial risk removes the primary supervisory tool used to pressure banks
      into dropping crypto clients — converting an informal enforcement posture
      into a rule-bound constraint that banks and their regulators can cite in
      future disputes.

    title: "Thirty-Nine State Banking Associations Launched an Industry-Owned Blockchain — 3,283 U.S. Banks Invited to Join"
    summary: >-
      On August 25, the BankChain Alliance was announced by a founding coalition
      spanning 3,283 community and regional banks across 39 U.S. states, holding
      $21.8 trillion in combined assets. The network is designed to carry tokenized
      commercial bank deposits, bank-issued stablecoins, smart payment tools, and
      automated settlement instruments on a permissioned shared ledger, with a
      target launch in 2027. Kathy Kraninger — former Consumer Financial Protection
      Bureau director and current CEO of the Florida Bankers Association — was named
      interim chair; the Alliance has not yet selected a technology partner and is
      conducting a formal procurement process before building begins. The Alliance
      cited $6.6 trillion in bank deposits that face direct competition from private
      stablecoin issuers as the strategic driver for building bank-controlled
      digital dollar infrastructure.
    consequence: >-
      An industry-owned, industry-governed network carrying tokenized deposits —
      controlled by the same banks that hold the underlying collateral — would
      position community and regional banks as the issuing infrastructure for
      digital dollars, rather than leaving that role to privately issued stablecoins
      by default.
